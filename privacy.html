Privacy

Tab Organizer — last updated 1 October 2026

Tab Organizer runs entirely inside your own copy of Chrome. Your tab groups, rules and settings are stored in your browser profile on your computer. There is no account, no server of ours, and no analytics. The only time anything leaves your computer is when you use an AI feature, and then it goes straight to the AI provider you chose — or, with Chrome's built-in model, nowhere at all.

What it reads

  • The title, address and tab group of your open tabs — that is the material it sorts.
  • The names and colours of your existing tab groups and windows.

It reads them when you ask it to — opening the popup or dashboard, running Classify, removing duplicates or merging groups — and in one other case: a small background part of the extension counts duplicate tabs when your tabs change, so the number on the toolbar icon and the popup's figures are ready. That count uses only the addresses of your open tabs, is kept in Chrome's temporary storage on your computer, and is never sent anywhere. The extension has no code that runs on the pages you visit.

What it stores, and where

Everything is kept in Chrome's own extension storage, on your computer, for that Chrome profile alone:

  • Your tab groups and their descriptions, including previous versions of them.
  • Your rules, plus counts of how often each one has matched.
  • Your settings, edited prompts, and your API key in encrypted form — along with the name you give the profile (such as “Personal”), which labels its backups.
  • A history of tabs you have classified — titles and addresses — which you can clear at any time in the dashboard.
  • Your most recent Classify run, until you apply it: the titles and addresses of the tabs it placed and the groups it proposed, so closing the dashboard first loses nothing. Applying, cancelling or discarding it deletes it, and the next run replaces it.
  • When the popup closes tabs, their addresses, places and group names for ten minutes, so Undo can reopen them. This is in Chrome's temporary storage, which is cleared when Chrome closes.
  • How long the popup took to open, the last 20 times. It is never included in a backup.

The extension never uses Chrome's sync storage, so none of this is copied to your other devices or your Google account by the extension. Removing the extension removes it all.

The only thing that is sent anywhere

When you run an AI feature — classifying tabs, building or editing a set of groups, or asking for a rule suggestion — the request contains:

  • the titles and addresses of the tabs in that request;
  • your group names and descriptions, so the model can choose between them;
  • the prompt text, which you can read and edit in the dashboard.

Where it goes depends on which of the three AI routes you set up:

  • Chrome's built-in model runs on your computer. The request never leaves it.
  • Your ChatGPT plan: the extension hands the request to a small helper you installed yourself, which runs OpenAI's Codex CLI on your computer; the CLI sends it to OpenAI under the ChatGPT account you signed it into. The extension never sees that account's credentials. Chrome asks your permission (“communicate with cooperating native applications”) before the extension can talk to the helper at all.
  • An API key: the extension sends the request directly from your browser to OpenAI or Anthropic, whichever the key is for, authenticated with that key.

Jev is optional, and only classifies. If you add a TypeSafe key, Classify first asks Jev, from TypeSafe AI, which of your groups each tab belongs in. For each tab, the extension sends its title, its site and its address (without the part after ? or #), plus your group names and descriptions, directly from your browser to TypeSafe, authenticated with your key. Only the tabs Jev is unsure of then go on to the AI route above. TypeSafe's handling of that data is covered by its privacy policy.

A request to OpenAI or Anthropic is covered by that provider's terms and privacy policy. With Auto, the extension uses the first of those routes you have set up, in the order key, ChatGPT, built-in. Nothing is sent if you set up none of them, and the rules-only parts of the extension work without any.

Your API key

Your key is encrypted in your browser with a passphrase you choose (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds, AES-GCM), and can be unlocked with Touch ID where your device supports it. It is decrypted only in the page that is making a request, never written anywhere in plain text, and never sent anywhere except to that provider as the request's credential.

Files you create

Backups, config exports and CSV exports are written to your Downloads folder when you ask for them. They contain your setup, and are as private as the folder you keep them in. An exported key travels only in its encrypted form, and your classify history and unapplied Classify run are included in a backup only if you tick the box for them; a config export never includes either.

What the developer receives

Nothing. No usage data, no crash reports, no tab data, no email address. There is no server to receive it.

Permissions, and why each one exists

  • tabs — to read the titles and addresses being sorted, and to move tabs into groups.
  • tabGroups — to create, rename, colour and merge tab groups.
  • storage — to keep your groups, rules and settings on your computer.
  • downloads — to save the backups and exports you ask for.
  • nativeMessaging (optional, asked for only when you connect ChatGPT) — to talk to the helper that runs the Codex CLI on your computer.
  • api.typesafe.ai (optional, asked for only when you save a TypeSafe key) — so Jev's requests go from the extension itself to TypeSafe.
  • api.openai.com, api.anthropic.com — so AI requests can be made from the extension itself, rather than through anyone else.

Children

Tab Organizer is a general-purpose productivity tool and is not directed at children.

Changes

If a future version sends anything anywhere else, this page will say so before that version ships, and the release notes will point at it. Questions or corrections: open an issue.
